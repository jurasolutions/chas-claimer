# Interview: chas-claimer

Ting Yu is away and delegated every decision ("make reasonable assumptions with crew and progress
this development until it is ready", 2026-11-13). The conductor ran the grilling as a design
tree, asked each round in full, and accepted the recommended answer for each question. Every
answer below is therefore an **assumption to confirm**, not a client fact. The ones that matter
most are collected in the PRD's Open Questions.

The client's own words: "Could you please share some examples of how you would extract the
Chas, Medisave, and Insurance Data from our database and submit it through your AI system?"

---

## Round 1: shape of the product

❓ **Q1 - Who uses it**: A dental clinic's claims are usually keyed by the front desk or a
clinic administrator, and a clinic manager or the dentist-owner signs off. Is the user a
**claims officer** who prepares and submits, with a **reviewer** (manager) who approves a
batch before it goes out?

➡️ Yes. Two roles: claims officer and reviewer. One clinic, possibly with several branches
(each branch has its own CHAS/MediSave registration, so branch is a field on every claim).

❓ **Q2 - Where it runs and where the clinic's data lives**: The clinic's database sits on
their own server or their clinic management system's cloud. A Cloudflare-hosted app cannot
reach a database on the clinic's LAN. Options: (a) run everything on a clinic PC; (b) host the
app on Cloudflare Pages + D1 and have a small **connector** at the clinic read their database
and push extracted records up; (c) ask the CMS vendor for an export.

➡️ (b), with (c) as an input the connector also accepts. The app runs on Cloudflare Pages
with D1, per the landing-page settings. The connector is a Node CLI that reads the clinic
database through a **source mapping** (SQL queries or a CSV export) and posts **source
records** to the app. For the first version the clinic database is a synthetic SQLite file
generated by a seed script, and the app can also load the same synthetic records directly so
the whole flow is demonstrable without the connector.

❓ **Q3 - How far automation goes**: Nothing is submitted without a person approving it?

➡️ Yes. Every claim passes the checks, sits in a queue, and is submitted only after a person
approves it (single claim or a batch). No auto-submit in the first version.

❓ **Q4 - Where AI earns its place**: Rules decide whether a claim is valid; they must be
deterministic and testable. AI is useful where text is messy: (1) suggesting the scheme's claim
code for a clinic's free-text treatment description; (2) explaining a payer rejection and
proposing the fix; (3) proposing a source mapping from the clinic database's schema during
onboarding. Accept these three, with every suggestion needing a person to accept it?

➡️ Yes. AI never decides eligibility or amounts. It only proposes; the person accepts or edits.
Nothing sent to the model carries an identifier (NRIC/FIN, name, phone, address, policy
number); only treatment text, codes, amounts and column names.

❓ **Q5 - Sign-in**: Patient identifiers and health data need access control. Build our own
login, or put **Cloudflare Access** in front and read the signed-in email for the audit trail?

➡️ Cloudflare Access (zero-code, supports Google/Microsoft/one-time PIN, MFA). The app reads
the `Cf-Access-Authenticated-User-Email` header for the actor. Roles come from an allowlist in
configuration. Local development uses a fixed demo user.

❓ **Q6 - Money**: Store every amount as integer cents in SGD?

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q7 - Audit trail**: An append-only event per state change and per submission attempt,
with actor and time, kept for the claim's life?

➡️ Yes. Payers and MOH audit clinics; the trail has to show who submitted what and when.

❓ **Q8 - Branding and name**: The app carries Jura's name (built by Jura) and is built from
the Jura design system. The clinic's name is configuration. Working product name "Claims desk".

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q9 - Public repo**: The GitHub repo is public. Keep only synthetic data in it and use an
NRIC prefix that cannot belong to a real person?

➡️ Yes. See the glossary's **synthetic identifier**. (Revised after the QA lead's review: the `S0000` block holds early real NRICs, so synthetic ones use the `T9` block, birth years 2090 to 2099. The Access JWT is verified, not just the email header.)

## Round 2: after the research (`docs/research/sg-dental-claims.md`)

The research changed the shape of the problem. CHAS claims go through MOH's Healthcare Claims
Portal (MHCP), logged in by a named person with Singpass under the clinic's Corppass. MediSave
goes through NPHC or MHCP, after the patient signs an MCAF. There is no public API or batch
upload for either; the only machine route is a clinic management system in Synapxe's SmartCMS
programme. From 13 Nov 2026 MHCP accepts Singapore IP addresses only. Insurers and TPAs each run
their own private panel channel and benefit codes.

❓ **Q10 - What "submit" means for CHAS and MediSave**: (a) a headless bot logs into MHCP with
stored credentials; (b) **assisted submission**: we prepare a checked claim packet and a
copy-ready checklist in the portal's order, a clinic user submits it in their own MHCP session,
and records the portal's claim reference back; (c) wait for SmartCMS.

➡️ (b). A bot needs shared Singpass credentials (warned against, possibly unlawful), runs into
Singpass 2FA, and is now IP-blocked from Cloudflare. Assisted submission is lawful today, and
the packet is the same data a future SmartCMS or browser-extension channel would need. (a) is
recorded as rejected in an ADR; (c) becomes a channel we can add later.

❓ **Q11 - The channel model**: Make the way a claim leaves the app a pluggable **submission
channel**, one per payer, chosen in settings: `assisted` (portal-assist checklist), `file`
(a batch file in the payer's column layout, for TPAs that take spreadsheets or statements),
`api` (a generic HTTPS endpoint with configurable URL and auth, for a TPA or CMS that offers
one), and `simulator` (our own stand-in payer, for demos and testing)?

➡️ Yes. All four share one interface: prepare a packet, send it (or hand it to a person), and
record the outcome. Real endpoints and credentials are keyed in later.

❓ **Q12 - Who decides a claim's outcome when there is no API**: Without an API, approvals,
rejections and payments only reach us when a person records them, or through a payment
statement import. Accept that outcomes are recorded by people (with the portal reference) or
by a **remittance import**, and the simulator plays the payer in demos?

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q13 - One visit, several payers**: A Merdeka Generation patient aged 62 gets a root canal.
CHAS pays its subsidy, Flexi-MediSave can pay part of the rest (up to $400 a year), and a
corporate dental plan may pay some of what remains. Model a **payer waterfall** per visit: CHAS
first, then MediSave, then insurance, then the patient pays the balance?

➡️ Yes. Each payer's claim is built on what the payer above it left. The order is
configuration (some insurers pay before MediSave by policy), defaulting to CHAS → MediSave →
insurance.

❓ **Q14 - Rules as data**: Subsidy amounts, frequency limits, the 30-day window, TOSP
rankings and withdrawal limits change (Oct 2025, Sep 2026, TOSP updates in 2026). Keep each
scheme's rules as a versioned **rule set** with a source URL and an effective date, picked by
visit date?

➡️ Yes. The first version ships the CHAS dental schedule effective 1 Oct 2025 and the TOSP
dental subset and day-surgery limits from the research note, each with its source.

❓ **Q15 - Limits we can't see**: MHCP holds the patient's real balance across every clinic.
Our counts only see this clinic's claims. Should an over-limit count block a claim?

➡️ No. Local limit counts produce a **warning** ("may exceed the annual limit, check the
balance in the portal"), never a block. Blocks are reserved for what is certain from the
record: wrong tier for dental (CHAS Green), card expired on the visit date, past the 30-day
window, a second CHAS claim for the same patient on the same day, an unfinished multi-visit
procedure, a MediSave claim without an MCAF, an MSP-ranked TOSP code.

❓ **Q16 - Above-benchmark fees**: From 1 Sep 2026 a CHAS claim for a fee above MOH's
benchmark needs a clinical justification. We only have two of the 18 benchmark ranges
verified. Make benchmarks configuration (blank until confirmed), flag fees above a configured
benchmark as needing a justification, and let AI draft one from the dentist's de-identified
notes for the dentist to edit?

➡️ Yes. The consultation and crown benchmarks ship with their source; the other 16 are left
blank for Ting Yu or the clinic to fill in.

❓ **Q17 - The clinic's own procedure codes**: Every clinic has its own treatment codes and
descriptions. Keep a **code map** per clinic: clinic code → CHAS procedure, TOSP code, and each
insurer's benefit code. When a treatment has no mapping, AI suggests one from the description,
and a person accepts it into the map?

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q18 - Getting data out of the clinic database**: The connector reads the clinic's
database with a **source mapping**: one SQL query per record type, returning the columns our
**source visit** needs. The first version supports SQLite files and CSV exports; other
databases (MySQL, SQL Server, Postgres) plug in behind the same seam once we know which one
the clinic runs. AI can propose the mapping from the database's column names (never its rows).

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q19 - Re-imports**: The connector will run daily and see the same visits again, sometimes
changed (a fee corrected, a crown marked complete). Treat each visit as identified by its
clinic visit ID; a re-import that changes a visit updates claims that haven't been submitted
yet, and flags a submitted claim as **changed after submission** instead of touching it?

➡️ Yes.

## Round 3: lifecycle, money and records

❓ **Q20 - Claim statuses**: `needs attention` (a blocking finding) → `ready` → `approved` →
`submitted` (with portal reference) → `accepted` or `rejected` → `paid` → done; `rejected` can
go back to `needs attention` or `ready` for a resubmission, or to `written off`. A claim can be
`withdrawn` before it's submitted. Accept?

➡️ Yes. Reconciled is not a status: a paid claim carries its remittance match.

❓ **Q21 - Who may do what**: Claims officers import, fix, submit and record outcomes.
Approval needs a reviewer, and the person who approves may be the person who prepared it (a
small clinic may have one person)?

➡️ Reviewer role required to approve; self-approval allowed but recorded. A clinic setting
can forbid it later.

❓ **Q22 - GST**: CHAS applies GST before the subsidy. Fees in the source record are taken as
the amount charged including GST when the branch is GST-registered?

➡️ Yes; the source record carries the fee before GST and the GST amount separately, and the
branch's GST flag decides.

❓ **Q23 - Public Assistance**: PA cardholders pay nothing for covered procedures; the
government reimburses the full fee. Treat PA as its own tier whose subsidy equals the fee?

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q24 - Rounding**: Subsidy is the lesser of the fee and the schedule's maximum, per line,
in cents, with no rounding needed beyond cents?

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q25 - Identifiers on screen**: Show NRIC/FIN masked (`S••••567D`) everywhere except the
assisted-submission checklist, where the full value is one click away for copying, and every
reveal is written to the audit trail?

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q26 - Remittance import**: Statement formats aren't public. Accept a CSV with a column
mapping per payer, match on portal claim reference first, then on patient + visit date +
amount, and leave everything else for a person to match by hand?

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q27 - Insurance specifics**: Insurance claims need the member's policy (member ID,
plan, employer), a benefit code per line, sometimes a tooth number and a pre-authorisation or
Letter of Guarantee reference. Annual benefit limits come from the policy record. Disclose
only claim lines, never clinical notes (MOH Circular 02/2026)?

➡️ Yes. The insurance packet builder only includes the fields the payer's template lists.

## Round 4: what's in the first version

❓ **Q28 - Scope of the first version**: In: the synthetic clinic database and the connector;
import; CHAS, MediSave (TOSP and Flexi-MediSave) and insurance claim building with the rules
above; the queue, claim detail and approval; the four channels with the assisted checklist,
file export, API and simulator; outcome recording; remittance import and matching; the AI
code suggestion, rejection explainer, justification draft and mapping assistant; settings for
clinic, branches, payers, channels and the code map; the audit trail. Out: a browser
extension, SmartCMS, Healthier SG, CDMP, multi-clinic tenancy, patient-facing anything,
email/notifications, real payer credentials.

➡️ Yes.

❓ **Q29 - Hosting and residency**: D1 with a location hint for Asia-Pacific; Cloudflare Access
in front; the clinic signs a data-intermediary agreement with Jura before any real data flows.

➡️ Yes; the agreement is listed as a prerequisite, not built.

and are listed in the PRD's Open Questions.
